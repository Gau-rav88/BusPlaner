## 3. Investigation of Date-Level and Vehicle-Run-Level Calibration Strategies

Milestone 4 investigated whether non-conformity residuals should be calibrated at the **vehicle-run** or **date** cluster level rather than at the individual segment level:

### 3.1 Mathematical Audit of Vehicle-Run Calibration on Validation Partition
In the validation partition (October 4), all 22 segment observations were generated by **3 distinct vehicles**:
1. `DL51EV3788`: 12 segments (mean residual $= 109.80\text{s}$, max residual $= 410.14\text{s}$).
2. `DL1PD5209`: 6 segments (mean residual $= 74.32\text{s}$, max residual $= 136.50\text{s}$).
3. `DL51EV3878`: 4 segments (mean residual $= 142.10\text{s}$, max residual $= 220.14\text{s}$).

### 3.2 Proof of Statistical Infeasibility with $n=3$ Clusters
To compute a distribution-free conformal quantile at coverage level $1 - \alpha = 0.90$ across $K$ clusters:
$$\text{Quantile Level } p = \frac{\lceil (K + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{K}$$
For $K = 3$ vehicle clusters:
$$p = \frac{\lceil (3 + 1) \times 0.90 \rceil}{3} = \frac{\lceil 3.6 \rceil}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.333 > 1.0$$

**Finding:** It is mathematically impossible to calibrate a $90\%$ conformal prediction interval across only 3 vehicle runs without parametric extrapolation. A valid non-parametric vehicle-run or date-level block conformal calibration requires at least **$K \ge 10\text{--}20$ independent clusters** ($\lceil (10+1) \times 0.9 \rceil / 10 = 10/10 = 1.0$).

## 4. Limitations of the Current Calibration Sample

1. **Exchangeability Assumptions Under Stress:**  
   Standard split-conformal prediction assumes exchangeability between calibration and test data. In our pilot:
   - Calibration occurred on **October 4 (Sunday)**, characterized by weekend free-flow traffic.
   - Testing occurred on **October 7 (Wednesday)**, characterized by weekday commuting, a major GPS feed outage, and severe evening peak congestion.
   - This temporal distribution shift explains why the empirical test coverage ($86.67\%$) fell slightly below the nominal $90\%$ target.
2. **Small Test Sample Size ($n_{\text{test}} = 30$):**  
   The illustrative $95\%$ confidence interval for coverage spans nearly 27 percentage points ($[69.28\%, 96.24\%]$).
3. **Strict Compliance Statement:**  
   **We do NOT claim guaranteed 90% conformal coverage.** The generated intervals provide transparent, calibrated pilot uncertainty bounds under DEGRADE status, but full empirical coverage guarantees require multi-week validation across larger date clusters.
